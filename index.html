The interviewer asks“Tell me about a time you found an error.”

What most people say

So, basically, at my last company we had many reports, and my manager used to ask me for different things, and one time there was an issue with the numbers, and actually it was a long process…

What we practice

Answer

I caught a duplicate billing entry before it reached the client.

Proof

I saw a variance in the monthly report, traced it to the entry and fixed it the same day.

Check

Would you like me to walk through how I found it?

An example answer, before and after coaching.

What we work on

Most answers fall short for the same few reasons. Each one is a habit, and habits can be trained.

A calm voice

A slower pace, pauses in place of “um” and “basically”, and a breath before you answer.

Confident wording

Saying “I” for your part of the work, without hedging or apologizing.

Direct answers

The answer in your first sentence, then one example, then a short check that you covered what they asked.

Stories that flow

Five stories from your own career that you can tell clearly in about 90 seconds each.

Inflection

Stressing the result, slowing down for the point, and ending statements like statements.

Camera presence

Eye contact through the lens, a natural smile and a setup that looks prepared.
